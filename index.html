Music tools

William Flynn

Small practice tools for jazz guitar, built for my students. Each one is a single page that works offline once loaded — open it in the practice room, phone in hand.

Jazz Guitar Technique Handbook3rd edition

The tools on this line are drawn from the handbook — same fingerings, same order, same terminology. They're meant to be used alongside the book, not instead of it.

  • Movable scale fingerings · pp. 6–23 Fretboardfretboard/ The movable scale fingerings — ten scales, every key, drawn in the book's orientation.
  • Shell Voicings 101 · pp. 45–51 Shell voicing buildershell-builder/ Build shell voicings by rule — roots low, 3rds and 7ths in the middle — then mobilize the top strings and see what chord results.
  • Drop-2 and Drop-3 · pp. 52–59 · key study p. 82 Inversion drillinversion-drill/ Drop-2 and drop-3 inversions on every string set, drilled up the neck, through the key studies — and through the leadsheet changes.
  • Scale Practice 101 · pp. 61–64 Scales deckscales-deck/ The practice concepts as a deck of cards: pick a key, a concept and a tier, and step through the exercises.
  • Arpeggio Practice · pp. 76–78 Arpeggios deckarpeggios-deck/ The full shape library on a browsable neck — by finger, or from any root you click — plus arpeggio practice three ways.

Stand-alone tools

These two aren't tied to the book.

  • 2 & 4two-and-four/ A time trainer that clicks on beats 2 and 4, then fades out bar by bar so your inner clock does the work.
  • Chartwrightchartwright/ A chord-chart editor with self-adjusting spacing — lyrics with the changes placed right over the syllables. Charts save on your device.
